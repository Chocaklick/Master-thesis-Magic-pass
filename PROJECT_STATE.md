# PROJECT STATE

## Project objective

Build a reproducible and scientifically defensible Business Analytics framework for Swiss ski destinations, integrating resort structure, hotel overnight stays, Magic Pass adoption, and climate/snow information. The authoritative mission is `prompts/main_research_prompt.md`.

## Current phase

Checkpoints 1–3 are complete and Checkpoints 4–5 formally fail for causal and heterogeneous-effect modelling under the present design. Checkpoint 6 is complete under Path C version 0.27: a seven-dimension exploratory evidence dashboard covers all 104 apparent candidates while keeping structural comparability, descriptive analogue opportunity, snow vulnerability, tourism dependence, accessibility, competition/network context and dated-evidence gaps separate. Its executable 19-group readiness registry permits only the three undated structural fields in the existing exploratory similarity calculation. It is not a causal or predictive uplift model: candidate coverage is never treated as model permission, the dashboard is sorted by stable cluster ID rather than an opportunity signal, zero candidates are approved for a complete Opportunity × Strategic Need matrix, and every pre-treatment, causal, recommendation and composite-score approval remains false. The integrated layers include reviewed tourism scopes, their transfer audit and a 0/5/10 km municipality-radius sensitivity, Swiss and cross-border population, NPVM context, stop and road proximity, scheduled winter-2026 journeys, current competing-pass/network context, an eight-season Top4 history, bounded Meilenweiss and Schneepass Zentralschweiz evidence, and tiered TopCard evidence for 2017/2018–2019/2020, plus cluster-geography and regional weather/snow sensitivities. The historical-pass layer documents positive presence for 21 candidates across four partially reconstructed products. TopCard core, three-day partner and area-specific summer access remain distinct; the unavailable 2022/2023 Schneepass season is not interpolated and all non-links remain unverified market absence. All layers remain qualified context rather than validated candidate catchments, complete historical market/access evidence or independent piste measurements.

## Current model decision

- Municipality fixed effects: diagnostic panel exists, but causal treatment/control status is not ready.
- Staggered DiD/event study: not credible yet.
- Matching/synthetic control: rejected for causal use under the present exhausted donor design.
- K-means plus nearest analogues: selected for exploratory Path C with stability and OOD diagnostics.
- Checkpoint 6 architecture: seven-dimension candidate evidence dashboard selected; no rank, complete strategic matrix, recommendation or composite score authorised.
- Causal forest, X-Learner, DR-Learner: unsupported by the effective treated sample.
- Gradient boosting/random forest: not run because no defensible cluster-level supervised target exists.
- Neural network: rejected; monthly repetition does not create independent resorts.

## Important methodological decisions

- Python remains the primary language; raw and external source responses are immutable.
- The existing lift clustering is a provisional reference layer and was not reconstructed.
- Resort listings, lift clusters, official Magic Pass destinations, reviewed destination units, and municipalities remain distinct units.
- A point's containing municipality is recorded only as `coordinate_container_only`; it is never promoted to tourism exposure.
- Membership events and destination-month rows remain causal-not-ready until treatment intensity/exposure, confounding, spillovers, and controls are approved.
- Crans-Montana's documented 2020-04-30 base-pass exit proves treatment cannot be assumed absorbing.
- Suppressed hotel values are missing, not zero, and are not imputed.
- Physically negative SLF snow-depth values are retained in raw evidence but set missing in derived metrics. Station proxies preserve distance/elevation diagnostics and never claim direct slope representation.
- SwissMetNet precipitation retains its official 06:00-to-06:00 UTC daily window. Weather anomalies use project-calculated 2013–2025 station/month references, not official climatological normals. Regional station proxies remain non-piste and causal-not-approved.
- Regional-weather station sensitivity replaces each component's selected SwissMetNet station with the next-nearest distinct station that passes the same inventory gates, monthly 80% coverage rule and station-specific 2013–2025 anomaly construction. It tests same-network station choice only; shared measurement design and spatial correlation mean it is not independent gridded validation, local piste weather or causal approval.
- Regional-weather grid sensitivity uses the nearest common-valid cell from the official 1 km TabsM/RhiresM LV95 grids, verifies every asset against its official STAC hash, and constructs 2013–2025 cell/month anomalies before destination aggregation. Grid spacing is not effective precipitation resolution; the products are station-informed spatial analyses with high-altitude and winter-precipitation limitations, not an independent measurement network, piste microclimate or causal approval.
- Gridded-snow sensitivity uses both resort-centred and selected-station-centred daily 3×3 medians from SPASS before November–April aggregation. This separates method disagreement from station-to-resort spatial mismatch without treating either source as ground truth. The distributed `HSCLQMD` variable lacks a units attribute; the explicit metres-to-centimetres interpretation is supported by physical magnitudes and the peer-reviewed HS definition, but remains an upstream metadata limitation. SPASS shares an SLF assimilation target and is not independent, piste-level, snowmaking/operation evidence or a causal approval.
- Validation must remain grouped by destination/municipality and time.
- Path C uses the existing lift cluster as the least duplicated available entity, while retaining the warning that it is not a certified independent destination. Membership, hotel outcomes, snow vulnerability and geography are excluded from the three-feature structural clustering.
- Analogue opportunity and snow vulnerability remain separate dimensions. Qualified point-container tourism, reviewed outcome-scope tourism, Swiss municipality-point and Eurostat cross-border grid population, NPVM zonal travel-time, current official stop-proximity, current FEDRO/ASTRA national-road-axis proximity, reviewed current scheduled-journey context, three scoped current competing-pass rosters and partial Top4/Meilenweiss/Schneepass history are available. The same-pair comparison shows that distance and scheduled time are correlated but not interchangeable. Approved candidate catchment-level tourism dependence, historically validated destination accessibility and full historical competition remain unavailable; strategic need is incomplete and no composite ranking is authorised.
- Point-container ratios and the separate reviewed-destination outcome-scope ratios are distinct products. The former describe only the municipality containing a directory point; the latter reproduce the exact municipalities used in the reviewed hotel outcome. Neither establishes a visitor catchment. The destination layer is also current/post-treatment and has no equivalent candidate scope, so it is excluded from analogue matching and the generic tourism-dependence field remains blank.
- Scope concordance is evaluated by exact BFS-set intersection, precision, recall and Jaccard, with definition-linked core proxies reported separately from complete observed scopes. A high global match rate is not transfer validation: the only multi-municipality complete scope exposes a missing municipality, so no candidate cluster inherits a reviewed outcome scope or catchment label.
- Feature availability and feature permission are governed separately. The 19-group registry recomputes candidate coverage, permits only the three undated structural fields in the existing exploratory similarity calculation, and requires every pre-treatment, causal, recommendation and composite-score approval to remain false.
- Checkpoint 6 assigns every readiness group once to seven separate dimensions and preserves all candidates in stable-ID order. The candidate dashboard may communicate qualified evidence and gaps, but it must not be sorted into a recommendation ranking or used to fill its intentionally blank uplift, confidence and composite fields.
- The Swiss population-threshold distances and radius totals use municipality representative points in EPSG:2056. A separate Eurostat 2021 grid supplies consistent Swiss/foreign one-kilometre cell-centroid totals; the two products are not combined because reference years and spatial allocation differ. Neither represents transport-network impedance, observed visitors or a destination catchment, so the generic accessibility score remains blank.
- NPVM travel times are assigned from exact listing-point traffic-zone containment where possible. High-alpine Swiss points outside the discontinuous zone polygons use only the nearest zone intersecting the same independently resolved current municipality; all 98 such links and distances are geometrically reconstructed. Fifty proxy-only clusters lack an internal exact-zone reference. The result is not live routing, rail-only access, a timetable, an arrival-node validation or a catchment, so it is never promoted to the generic accessibility score.
- Public-transport-stop proximity retains only official `VP`/`VPG` passenger stops. Train stations require official elementary mode code `B`; rack railway code `E` is not silently relabelled. Distances are straight-line from supplied listing points, not routes, timetable/frequency quality, seasonal service, valley-station access or catchments. All-mode nearest stops can be cableways or chairlifts, so the result is never promoted to the generic accessibility score.
- National-road proximity uses the official `Stammachsen` main-axis layer separately from the union of main, feeder, connection and ramp axes. The published schema contains no motorway-class or usable-entrance field, so these are current straight-line point-to-line diagnostics—not motorway access, a legal route, travel time, winter reliability or historical infrastructure—and are never promoted to the generic accessibility score.
- Competing-pass status is resolved first at the supplied listing level through a versioned 43-area register. AlpsPass unlimited core access is not equated with its three-free-ski-days partner benefit, mixed lift clusters remain partial, and a no-link value means only absence of an explicit link in the three reviewed rosters. The 2026/2027/current snapshots are descriptive business context, not historical pre-treatment competition or verified market absence.
- Historical Top4, Meilenweiss, Schneepass Zentralschweiz and TopCard status is resolved separately at area-season, product-listing-season and preserved-cluster levels. TopCard additionally separates unlimited core access from three-day partners and explicit area-level summer access from product-wide statements. Conservative entity links deliberately omit unproven subcomponents: Malbun is not confused with Buchserberg-Malbun, Hoher Kasten remains unmatched, Airolo-Pesciüm is not widened to Airolo-Lüina and Schatzalp is not inherited from Davos-Klosters. The unavailable Schneepass 2022/2023 season is not interpolated. The evidence establishes positive presence only; four partial products cannot establish full historical market coverage or absence, and several observations postdate Magic Pass anchors.
- Spatial network context excludes the focal cluster and keeps all-directory, current Magic, reviewed competing-pass and union counts separate at 25 and 50 km. It uses great-circle distance between cluster-median supplied listing coordinates; it is not terrain-aware travel, an independent commercial-domain inventory, historical market penetration, skier substitution or a causal network feature.
- Cluster-geography sensitivity preserves every existing assignment and compares minimum, median and maximum cross-listing-point distances with the coordinate-wise-median baseline. Minimum is permissive, maximum is conservative and no rule is uniquely correct; the audit does not certify geographic/commercial coherence or approve a model feature.
- Scheduled public-transport journeys use checksum-verified GTFS Static, normalized Swiss base stop IDs, active service calendars, generic published transfers and explicit six-vehicle/eight-hour limits. Initial waiting and in-vehicle journey time remain separate. A weekday and Saturday snapshot does not represent real-time disruptions, fares, crowding, every seasonal day or historical service stability, so the measure is descriptive and never promoted to a causal accessibility feature.
- Destination arrival-node review requires an exact versioned component/stop match, bus/train capability, the component point's current municipality and a maximum 2.1 km point distance. The six village/local-centre fallbacks remain labelled as such. This review does not establish door-to-lift walking, historical node continuity or line stability.
