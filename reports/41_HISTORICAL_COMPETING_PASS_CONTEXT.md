# Historical competing-pass context: Top4, Meilenweiss, Schneepass Zentralschweiz, TopCard and SnowPass Graubünden

## Evidence recovered

The checksum-pinned Top4 reconstruction remains unchanged: four official regions over eight consecutive seasons from 2017/2018 through 2024/2025. Three official operator PDFs retain the bounded Meilenweiss evidence for 2019/2020, 2020/2021 and 2021/2022. Schneepass Zentralschweiz now covers four consecutive seasons from 2021/2022 through 2024/2025: the previously missing 2022/2023 season is recovered from checksum-pinned official winter and summer pages captured on 30 November 2022. The archived winter page supplies the complete 15-area roster and the separate SchneepassPlus page documents a 13-area summer product valid through 30 September 2023; the product-wide summer statement is not widened into area-specific rights.

Three official TopCard sources add 2017/2018 through 2019/2020. Samnaun/Ischgl, Davos-Klosters and Flims-Laax form the 2017/2018 core. Arosa Lenzerheide replaces Samnaun/Ischgl in 2018/2019. The three core areas remain unlimited, while the additional partner benefit is kept in a separate tier: three freely selectable days in Engadin St. Moritz/Oberengadin in 2018/2019, replaced by three visits at Engelberg-Titlis from 2019/2020. The supplied Schatzalp listing is not inferred from the broader Davos-Klosters label.

SnowPass Graubünden is now separated from both TopCard and Schneepass Zentralschweiz. The official Samnaun tariff supports one exact 2017/2018 host-area link. For 2018/2019, the BBGR report proves the product and aggregate company groups but names no operators; a separate joint annual report by Corvatsch AG and Diavolezza Lagalb AG records that the Engadin would retain SnowPass Graubünden for frequent skiers. Only the two exact host listings of those reporting operators are linked, not the wider Engadin roster. For 2019/2020, an immutable capture of the official Graubünden Ferien page states winter-only validity at all Graubünden mountain railways including Samnaun-Ischgl and Sedrun-Andermatt, while the industry study independently names 17 major Graubünden areas. Those sources support **20 conservative area-season rows** and **23 positive listing-season links**. The linked set is deliberately not called complete because the 2018/2019 named roster and the referenced 2019/2020 detailed validity PDF remain unavailable, and individual small-lift exclusions remain unresolved.

The combined output contains **138 area-season rows**, **5691 product-listing-season rows** and **164 positive listing-season links**. These reach **37 lift clusters**, including **30 of 104** apparent Path C candidates.

## Winter and summer are not conflated

Each linked area is positive winter evidence. TopCard contributes **18 unlimited-core** and **2 limited-partner** listing-season links; those tiers are never pooled as equivalent access. Its three core areas have documented summer access, and the 2019/2020 Engelberg-Titlis benefit is limited to three visits shared between summer and winter. This yields **10 area-season** and **19 listing-season** summer links, all from TopCard.

Meilenweiss and Schneepass Zentralschweiz retain product-level summer statements where their official material supports them. Their aggregate statements do not identify every area-level summer entitlement, so no area-specific summer right is inferred for those products. SnowPass Graubünden is explicitly retained as winter-only. Product-level and area-specific evidence remain separate.

## Conservative scope and negative evidence

Malbun in Liechtenstein is not linked to the supplied Buchserberg-Malbun listing, which is a different Swiss area. Hoher Kasten has no equivalent supplied ski listing. The historical Airolo-Pesciüm label is not widened to Airolo-Lüina, and broad cluster neighbours never inherit a documented listing link. The official Pizol 2018/2019 annual report was checksum-verified, text-searched and visually reviewed but contains no explicit Meilenweiss reference; it is recorded as a non-confirming source and creates neither membership nor absence evidence.

## Decision limit

Five products and twenty-one product-seasons still do not reconstruct the full historical multi-resort-pass market. The recovered 2022/2023 Schneepass Zentralschweiz roster is season-specific official evidence rather than an adjacent-season interpolation; the two source-specific 2018/2019 SnowPass Graubünden host links are not widened to the unnamed remainder of that season's roster; non-links do not verify absence; evidence scopes differ by season; several observations postdate Magic Pass treatment anchors; and benefit intensity and prices are not harmonised. Historical-market-absence, pre-treatment, causal, recommendation and composite-score approvals remain false. The result is qualified context, not a ranking input.
