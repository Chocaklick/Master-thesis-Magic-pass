# 03 — Data quality

## Overall assessment

The supplied files are structurally stronger than their filenames initially suggested: hashes are stable, identifiers reconcile, lift assignments are geographically reproducible, and the hotel panel preserves suppression rather than silently replacing it. The main risks are semantic and causal rather than mechanical.

## Resort-level assessment

`data_processed/resort_data_quality.csv` scores eight equally weighted evidence dimensions: identity scope, lift assignment, geolocation, tourism exposure, hotel outcome, membership history, infrastructure completeness, and snow/climate. After the qualified SLF proxy was added, scores range from 28.1 to 68.8, with a mean of 52.3. Low-comparability snow links receive only partial credit.

This is an evidence-completeness score, not a resort ranking, opportunity score, or model confidence interval. All rows remain `causal_ready = false`, and each unresolved component is exposed in `data_quality_warnings`.

## Highest-priority corrections

1. Validate treatment intensity and exact winter-versus-summer scope. All active seasons are documented for the 14 reviewed units and seven panel units pass the 24/24 coverage screen, but no causal treatment or control is approved (see `08_MEMBERSHIP_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md`).
2. Rebuild the donor search if causal identification remains a goal. The current top-five-per-treatment shortlist is exhausted: 28 pairs are proximity-deprioritised and all seven remote pairs are excluded for overlapping local shocks. Complete-roster station-label absence is documented for the remote pairs, although corporate-affiliate and exact competing-pass tariff details remain qualified.
3. Convert the completed conservative 26-label scope review into independently validated destination domains. Four documented Swiss destinations have no own supplied-directory row, and Les Diablerets remains an explicit many-to-many overlap rather than an identity match.
4. Establish tourism-exposure/accommodation markets and audit spillovers without double-counting. The STATPOP/HESTA ratios describe point containers only. The Eurostat grid now removes the cross-border geographic truncation of population radii, but residential straight-line totals still do not define skier demand or visitor catchments; NPVM values can also use municipality-constrained zone proxies. Current ASTRA national-road-axis proximity is available, but the source does not classify motorways or usable entrances and the geometry is not a route. Winter-2026 GTFS supplies scheduled journey time from municipality main stops to 15 component arrival nodes whose current name/mode/municipality/distance review is complete. Six are explicit village/local-centre proxies, door-to-lift walking is unverified, and the 2018–2025 archive feeds require manual or authorised acquisition before identical stop/service comparison. Within destination, distance and scheduled round-trip ranks have a median correlation of 0.743 across ten calculable destinations, but 225 pairs have materially worse transit rank than distance suggests. The same-destination distance gradient remains exploratory: six of seven near/far contrasts are positive, but no exposure or effect is approved.
5. Audit historical BFS municipality changes before panel estimation. The seven STATPOP 2025 identifiers affected by the five official 2026 mutations are now reconciled to current geometry with the population total preserved, but the earlier outcome panel is not fully boundary-harmonised.
6. Keep the completed climate sensitivities qualified. The next-nearest SLF-station test has a 47.2% median relative absolute depth gap. The 1 km SPASS snow audit shows high/moderate agreement for all 15 components when the model is sampled around the station, versus eight low comparisons and a 61.9% median relative gap when sampled around the resort; station placement/elevation is therefore a central representativeness problem. SPASS shares an SLF snow-assimilation target and is not independent. For temperature and precipitation, the official 1 km TabsM/RhiresM comparison has high destination-level agreement for eight units, moderate for one and low for Axalp and Meiringen-Hasliberg because their valley-station temperature anomalies diverge from the resort-grid signal. A genuinely separate measurement or reanalysis source is still required only if a climate variable is proposed as a causal covariate.
